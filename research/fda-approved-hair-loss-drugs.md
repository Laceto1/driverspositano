# Which drugs are FDA-approved specifically for treating hair loss, and for which type of hair loss?

**Date:** 2026-11-14. Research summary from a fact-checked agent swarm.

## Bottom line

- **Androgenetic alopecia (pattern hair loss) has two FDA-approved drugs.**
  - **Finasteride 1 mg (Propecia)** is approved for **male** androgenetic alopecia only. It was approved Dec 19, 1997. Proscar 5 mg was approved June 19, 1992 for a different indication. These dates come from the FDA compounding alert page.
  - **Topical minoxidil** is sold over the counter for androgenetic alopecia in **men and women**, on the vertex (top of the scalp), under FDA drug labeling. The original approval dates were not verified.
- **Severe alopecia areata has three FDA-approved drugs, all oral JAK inhibitors:**
  - **Baricitinib (Olumiant):** adults since June 13, 2022. The label was later expanded to adolescents 12 and older (see the next point).
  - **Ritlecitinib (Litfulo):** ages 12 and up, approved June 23, 2023. Pfizer's press release and trade coverage dated June 26, 2023 date it to June 2023.
  - **Deuruxolitinib (Leqselvi):** adults only, approved July 25, 2024. Sun Pharma announced it the same day.
- **The baricitinib approval for adolescents is real, but its exact date is not settled.** Lilly's press release, reprinted on placera.se, reports Sept 25, 2026. Some outlets give Sept 28–30. The adolescent indication appears in a label revised in Oct 2026, and no source confirmed the approval date separately.
- **All three alopecia areata drugs carry the JAK inhibitor class boxed warning.** It covers serious infections, death, cancer, major cardiovascular events (MACE) and blood clots. The current labels also address older adults. Check this before choosing any of the three.
- **These are not FDA-approved for hair loss:**
  - **Low-dose oral minoxidil** is popular but off-label for every type of hair loss in the US.
  - **Compounded topical finasteride**, including telehealth products, is not FDA-approved, so FDA has not evaluated its safety, efficacy or quality. The FDA alert said to be dated April 22, 2025 is (unconfirmed): it was seen only in news reports.
- **Chemotherapy-induced alopecia has no FDA-approved drug.** Only scalp-cooling devices are available, and those were cleared as medical devices (De Novo/510(k)), not approved as drugs. The other hair-loss types with no approved drug are listed in the source material, but the names were not passed into this summary.
- **No new androgenetic alopecia drug was approved in 2025–2026** (unconfirmed). This was inferred from search results, sponsor guidance and trade press. FDA's Novel Drug Approvals lists and Drugs@FDA could not be opened.
- **Pipeline: nothing below is approved for hair loss.**
  - **Clascoterone 5% topical solution (Cosmo, formerly "Breezula")** is for male androgenetic alopecia. The NDA has not been filed, and company guidance targets early 2027. There is no PDUFA date.
  - Its SCALP 1 and SCALP 2 Phase III topline results (Dec 3, 2025) and 12-month extension data (Apr 15, 2026) are sponsor-reported (unconfirmed).
  - **Pyrilutamide (KX-826, Kintor)** is investigational in the US. A Chinese marketing application is in progress (unconfirmed).
  - **PP405 (Pelage)** is in Phase 2/3 with no NDA (unconfirmed).
- **What to do first:** match the drug to the diagnosis. Pattern hair loss: topical minoxidil (men or women) or finasteride 1 mg (men only). Severe alopecia areata: one of the three JAK inhibitors, within the age limits above, after weighing the boxed warning.

### Evidence grading: androgenetic alopecia

| name | details | status | evidence | check | sources |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Comparative efficacy in male AGA: network meta-analysis and EDF S3 guideline | Gupta et al., JAMA Dermatology 2022: Bayesian network meta-analysis of 23 studies of minoxidil, finasteride and dutasteride monotherapy in men. At 24 weeks, dutasteride 0.5 mg/d gave the largest total hair count gain (MD +7.1 hairs/cm2 vs finasteride 1 mg), and oral minoxidil 5 mg beat finasteride 1 mg on terminal hair count (MD +10.4/cm2). The single item checked out against the abstract: 23 studies were included, and the 24-week dutasteride advantage (MD 7.1/cm2 vs finasteride) was confirmed. The ranking depends on the time point. Dutasteride and oral minoxidil lead at 24 weeks, while finasteride 1 mg leads on terminal hair at 48 weeks. Most of these comparisons are indirect and most trials were small, and the network includes off-label agents (dutasteride, oral minoxidil). That limits how far the high evidence grade should be taken.

### Hype check

| name | details | status | evidence | check | sources |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Low-dose oral minoxidil: popular but off-label for hair loss | Meta-analysis of 4 RCTs (279 patients, 24-39 weeks) comparing oral minoxidil with topical minoxidil solution in AGA (Int J Dermatol 2025 Mar, epub Oct 2024). Both popular telehealth hair-loss options sit outside FDA approval.
