# Hair Restoration Doctors and Cutting-Edge Treatments for Thinning Hair

**Date:** 2026-11-14. Research summary, not medical advice.

## 1. Bottom line

- **Diagnose before treating.** Iron, thyroid and CBC screening is standard for women with diffuse shedding or thinning. In a 2025 study of 2,851 women with TE, ferritin was low in 1,123 of the 2,413 tested (46.5%). Ferritin thresholds, and whether supplements help, are still debated ([1](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39950230/), [2](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11826290/), [3](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26436027/); check: [C1](https://eutils.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/entrez/eutils/efetch.fcgi?db=pubmed&rettype=abstract&retmode=text&id=39950230)). Postpartum shedding usually resolves by 6-12 months. In a study of 200 women with postpartum hair loss, only 9.5% had TE alone; most also had AGA and/or traction alopecia ([1](https://www.news-medical.net/health/Postpartum-Hair-Loss-Causes-Timeline-and-When-to-Worry.aspx), [2](https://jcadonline.com/1941-2789-17-5-15/), [3](https://www.ccjm.org/content/ccjom/93/10/616.full.pdf); check: [C1](https://eutils.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/entrez/eutils/efetch.fcgi?db=pubmed&rettype=abstract&retmode=text&id=38779373,42823125)).

- **Men, what works now.** Finasteride 1 mg and topical minoxidil are FDA-approved. Dutasteride 0.5 mg is off-label, but two network meta-analyses (Gupta 2022 and 2024) ranked it highest for hair density (unverified) ([1](https://hcplive.com/view/minoxidil-5-a-reductase-inhibitors-relatively-androgenetic-alopecia), [2](https://medicaldialogues.in/dermatology/news/study-details-relative-efficacy-of-different-drugs-for-treating-androgenic-alopecia-88088), [3](https://pesquisa.bvsalud.org/portal/resource/es/mdl-38725143); check: none given). Low-dose oral minoxidil is off-label but widely prescribed. A 1,404-patient safety study found hypertrichosis in 15.1% and only 1.7% stopped treatment. In the 2024 RCT (90 enrolled, 68 completed), oral 5 mg was **not superior** to topical 5%, so it is an alternative for people who cannot use topical minoxidil, not a stronger drug ([1](https://www.pubmed.gov/33639244/), [2](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamadermatology/fullarticle/2817326), [3](https://speciality.medicaldialogues.in/in-female-pattern-hair-loss-low-dose-oral-minoxidil-as-effective-as-topical-minoxidil-jaad); check: [C1](https://eutils.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/entrez/eutils/efetch.fcgi?db=pubmed&rettype=abstract&retmode=text&id=33639244,38598226,35107565), [C2](https://clinicaltrials.gov/api/v2/studies/NCT06924632)).

- **Women, what works now.** All of these are off-label.
  - Oral spironolactone is the common first-line systemic anti-androgen. About 57% improve in pooled data, more on combination therapy, and women who can become pregnant need contraception ([1](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10502763/), [2](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37719557/), [3](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10010138/); check: [C1](https://eutils.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/entrez/eutils/efetch.fcgi?db=pubmed&rettype=abstract&retmode=text&id=37719557,36923692,29231239)).
  - Oral minoxidil 1 mg was compared with topical 5% in 52 women. Density gains were similar (12% vs 7.2%, not significant), and hypertrichosis was more common on the oral drug (27% vs 4%) ([1](https://speciality.medicaldialogues.in/in-female-pattern-hair-loss-low-dose-oral-minoxidil-as-effective-as-topical-minoxidil-jaad), [2](https://repositorio.unesp.br/handle/11449/191018), [3](https://mdedge.com/content/low-dose-oral-minoxidil-treatment-alopecia); check: PubMed 31473295 and 33639244, [C1](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC13594684/fullTextXML)).
  - Compounded topical finasteride was as safe and effective as topical minoxidil in a 45-woman RCT ([1](https://doaj.org/article/596e9f49c813461f9946997e0515acea), [2](https://advances.umw.edu.pl/en/article/2023/32/7/813/), [3](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30206824/); check: [C1](https://eutils.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/entrez/eutils/efetch.fcgi?db=pubmed&rettype=abstract&retmode=text&id=40117614,30206824), [C2](https://clinicaltrials.gov/api/v2/studies?query.term=finasteride+spironolactone+postmenopausal+minoxidil)).
  - Evidence for oral finasteride after menopause is mixed. The 1 mg RCT in 137 women showed no benefit, while small series at 2.5-5 mg reported improvement ([1](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15459533/), [2](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3746222), [3](https://www.dovepress.com/finasteride-and-its-potential-for-the-treatment-of-female-pattern-hair-peer-reviewed-fulltext-article-DDDT); check: [C1](https://eutils.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/entrez/eutils/efetch.fcgi?db=pubmed&rettype=abstract&retmode=text&id=11050579,15459533,23960392,29464847)).

- **Coming: VDPHL01 (oral extended-release minoxidil, Veradermics).** It is investigational and not FDA-approved. Two trials in men were positive:
  - Study 302 reported 12-month data in November 2026.
  - Phase 3 Study 304 reported topline results on October 19, 2026: +28.6 hairs/cm² once daily and +36.4 twice daily, vs +6.6 on placebo.

  An NDA is expected. The women's Study 306 (NCT07146022, 552 estimated, no longer enrolling) reports topline data in the first half of 2027. All figures are company-reported, and the material gives no approval date ([1](https://www.biospace.com/press-releases/veradermics-achieved-the-highest-vdphl01-reported-hair-count-in-the-phase-3-program-to-date-in-positive-12-month-study-302-data-in-male-pattern-hair-loss), [2](https://finance.yahoo.com/healthcare/articles/veradermics-oral-vdphl01-achieved-early-110000656.html), [3](https://www.biospace.com/press-releases/veradermics-reports-third-quarter-2026-financial-results-and-highlights-recent-corporate-and-clinical-progress), [4](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260715636372/en/Veradermics-Oral-VDPHL01-Delivers-Positive-Study-207-Phase-2-Clinical-Trial-Results-in-Female-Pattern-Hair-Loss-Ahead-of-Upcoming-Phase-23-Study-306-Readout), [5](https://ir.veradermics.com/news-releases/news-release-details/veradermics-reports-second-quarter-2026-financial-results-and); check: [C1](https://clinicaltrials.gov/api/v2/studies?query.term=VDPHL01), [C2](https://www.fiercepharma.com/pharma/veradermics-hot-streak-continue-another-phase-3-win-against-hair-loss), [C3](https://clinicaltrials.gov/api/v2/studies/NCT07146022), [C4](https://clinicaltrials.gov/api/v2/studies/NCT06527365)).

- **Coming: other drugs in development.** None is approved for hair loss.
  - **Clascoterone 5% solution** is a topical anti-androgen. Both Phase 3 trials are completed (1,465 men), and an NDA and EU MAA are planned. The 539% and 168% improvements over vehicle are topline figures, not peer-reviewed ([1](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT05910450), [2](https://practicaldermatology.com/news/clascoterone-5-solution-acheives-hair-regrowth-in-large-aga-study/2484730/), [3](https://www.healio.com/news/dermatology/20260422/male-pattern-hair-loss-drug-regrows-hair-up-to-1-year-without-plateau), [4](https://ishrs.org/ishrs-world-congress-2026/), [5](https://www.hcplive.com/view/sustained-1-year-efficacy-seen-clascoterone-androgenetic-alopecia-maria-hordinsky-md); check: [C1](https://clinicaltrials.gov/api/v2/studies/NCT05910450), [C2](https://clinicaltrials.gov/api/v2/studies/NCT05914805), [C3](https://www.genengnews.com/topics/translational-medicine/stockwatch-cosmo-surges-40-on-baldness-candidates-phase-iii-data/), [C4](https://clinicaltrials.gov/api/v2/studies?query.cond=androgenetic+alopecia&query.term=clascoterone), [C5](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/scripts/cder/daf/index.cfm?event=overview.process&ApplNo=213433), [C6](https://www.newsfilecorp.com/release/292660/)).
  - **PP405 (Pelage):** the men's Phase 2/3 AWAKEN (NCT07819435, 600 men) has been recruiting since September 21, 2026. The women's Phase 2 AWAKENING (NCT07907783) was first posted October 30, 2026. The Phase 2a gave the drug for only 4 weeks and has no peer-reviewed efficacy data ([1](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20250617338859/en/Pelage-Pharmaceuticals-Announces-Positive-Phase-2a-Clinical-Trial-Results-for-PP405-in-Regenerative-Hair-Loss-Therapy), [2](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07907783), [3](https://cosmedica.com/pp405-hair-loss-treatment/), [4](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT06393452), [5](https://pelagepharma.com/press-releases/pelage-pharmaceuticals-announces-positive-phase-2a-clinical-trial-results-for-pp405-in-regenerative-hair-loss-therapy/); check: [C1](https://clinicaltrials.gov/api/v2/studies/NCT07819435), [C2](https://clinicaltrials.gov/api/v2/studies/NCT06393452), [C3](https://clinicaltrials.gov/api/v2/studies/NCT07907783)).
  - **GT20029 (Kintor):** the published China Phase II in 180 men was positive. The 1% twice-weekly dose was chosen for Phase III, and a US Phase II is planned ([1](https://www.dermatologytimes.com/view/first-in-class-topical-gt20029-demonstrates-promising-phase-2-efficacy-and-tolerability-for-aga), [2](https://www.biospace.com/gt20029-china-phase-ii-trial-for-aga-reached-primary-endpoint); check: [C1](https://eutils.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/entrez/eutils/efetch.fcgi?db=pubmed&rettype=abstract&retmode=text&id=41328006)).

### Proven now
- **Finasteride 1 mg and topical minoxidil** are FDA-approved for male AGA. **Dutasteride 0.5 mg** is off-label but ranked highest for hair density in network meta-analyses (Gupta 2022, 23 studies; Gupta 2024). (unverified) [source](https://hcplive.com/view/minoxidil-5-a-reductase-inhibitors-relatively-androgenetic-alopecia)
- **Low-dose oral minoxidil** is off-label but widely prescribed, with a 1,404-patient safety record (JAAD 2021). In the 2024 RCT (90 enrolled, 68 completed), oral 5 mg was **not superior** to topical 5%. It is an alternative for people who cannot use topical minoxidil, not a stronger drug. [source](https://www.pubmed.gov/33639244/), [source](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamadermatology/fullarticle/2817326)
- **Oral spironolactone** is the common first-line systemic anti-androgen for women. About 57% improve in pooled data, more when combined with other treatments. [source](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37719557/)
- **Diagnose first.** Iron, thyroid and CBC screening is standard: low ferritin appeared in 46.5% of 2,413 women tested in a 2025 study. Postpartum TE usually resolves by 6-12 months, and shedding that lasts longer can reveal underlying FPHL. [source](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39950230/), [source](https://jcadonline.com/1941-2789-17-5-15/)
- **Surgery and repair** follow ISHRS FUE (2019) and FUT (2024) guidelines (unverified). Body- and beard-hair FUE is an established repair technique for depleted donor areas (unverified). [source](https://ishrs.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/fut-task-force-nov-1-2024.pdf), [source](https://ishrs.org/patients/beard-mustache-transplant/)

### Strong newer options
- **LDOM in women:** in 52 women (Ramos, JAAD 2020), density gains were similar to topical minoxidil (12% vs 7.2%, not significant), with more hypertrichosis (27% vs 4%). [source](https://speciality.medicaldialogues.in/in-female-pattern-hair-loss-low-dose-oral-minoxidil-as-effective-as-topical-minoxidil-jaad)
- **Topical finasteride in women:** a 45-woman RCT (2025) found it as safe and effective as topical minoxidil. It is small and only available compounded. [source](https://doaj.org/article/596e9f49c813461f9946997e0515acea)
- **Oral finasteride after menopause:** evidence is mixed. The 1 mg RCT (137 women) showed no benefit; small uncontrolled series at 2.5-5 mg reported improvement. [source](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15459533/)
- **FFA:** dutasteride-based multimodal therapy stabilized about 63% of 366 patients in non-randomized studies. No RCTs exist. [source](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6500869)

### Hype or unproven (and why)
- **Metformin for CCCA** (unverified): the only sources are media coverage. There is no RCT and it is not standard therapy. [source](https://thegrio.com/2024/09/26/how-a-doctor-at-johns-hopkins-has-been-using-diabetes-medication-to-treat-alopecia)
- **PP405 efficacy claims:** the drug was given for only 4 weeks, the 31% figure comes from secondary coverage, nothing is peer-reviewed, and efficacy in women is not established. [source](https://cosmedica.com/pp405-hair-loss-treatment/)
- **Clascoterone's 539% and 168% headlines:** these are relative improvements over vehicle from topline company data. ## 4. How to choose a surgeon, and red flags

This section comes only from web-search result summaries. The researchers could not open the ishrs.org pages directly, and they could not query the ABHRS or IAHRS directories. Check each point against the source yourself before you rely on it (see section 5). Items marked "(unverified)" had no usable source link in the research notes.

### Credentials to check

- **ABHRS Diplomate status.** ABHRS was founded in 1996. To become a Diplomate, a surgeon must pass written and oral exams and document their surgical experience. Check a surgeon's status in the official ABHRS physician directory, "Find a Diplomate", at [abhrs.org/directory](https://abhrs.org/directory), or contact the ABHRS office at info@abhrs.org. The office address in Munster, Indiana comes from an older 2018 candidate brochure ([PDF](https://abhrs.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/08/Candidate-Brochure-2018-V2.pdf)), so it may be out of date.
- **ABHRS is not an ABMS member board.** [certificationmatters.org](https://www.certificationmatters.org) will not show ABHRS status. Check ABHRS status at abhrs.org, and check any specialty board status separately at certificationmatters.org.
- **How ABHRS status should be described.** The ABHRS ethics policy says Diplomates must use the term "ABHRS Diplomate", not "board certified". A clinic that calls ABHRS status "board certified" is going against that policy. (unverified: the link to the policy page was cut off in the research notes)
- **ISHRS membership and fellowship (FISHRS).** The researchers confirmed ISHRS status through ishrs.org doctor-profile pages (ishrs.org/doctor/...) and the FISHRS roster at [ishrs.org/fishrs](https://ishrs.org/fishrs), dated Oct 22, 2025. If a surgeon is listed as ISHRS "Emeritus", they may be doing fewer surgeries.
- **IAHRS membership** ([iahrs.org](https://iahrs.org), founded 2001). (unverified: the notes give no source page for these points)
  - Membership is by invitation and review. It is not open to every hair transplant doctor.
  - Surgeons must show results photos from their own cases and agree to unannounced inspections of their facility.
  - IAHRS investigates complaints and can end a membership for misconduct.
  - IAHRS publishes no detailed checklist of how it reviews applications.
- **Academic titles can be out of date.** Search results sometimes showed old titles and roles. For example, Cotsarelis's term as Penn dermatology chair ended in 2025, but some Penn pages still list him as chair. Mount Sinai's hair-clinic leadership has also changed: older items name Guttman-Yassky as director, while 2026 sources name Ungar. Check the institution's current faculty page.
- **Turkey: TSHRS (Turkish Society of Hair Restoration Surgeons).** The researchers found no official website or member directory, so they could not check anyone's membership. The only evidence about its leadership is clinic and aggregator pages that name Dr. Tayfun Oguzoglu as president or chairman since 2020. (unverified)

### Red flags

- **Technician-performed surgery.** ISHRS or IAHRS membership does not prove that the surgeon performs the surgery at that clinic. ISHRS advises asking in writing who makes the incisions and who harvests the grafts. (unverified: the source link for this ISHRS advice was cut off in the research notes)
- **Paid membership is not proof of skill.** Surgeons accepted into IAHRS pay annual dues. IAHRS says paying the dues does not guarantee 12 months of membership. The researchers found no dollar amount. The research did not look at other paid or advertising-based directories. (unverified)
- **"Board certified" used for ABHRS status.** See the ABHRS wording rule above.
- **Conflicts of interest.** Check whether a doctor has a financial stake in a treatment they recommend. For example, Rodney Sinclair has a disclosed patent and stock interest in oral minoxidil for hair loss (Samson Medical). Most of his evidence on low-dose oral minoxidil comes from small, single-center or observational studies. (unverified: the notes give no source link)
- **Unrealistic graft counts.** The research did not cover this. The track that was meant to cover graft survival and surgical outcomes ran no searches, so this report has no sourced figures to compare a quote against.

## 5. Caveats and what to verify before booking

**This is research, not medical advice.** It is a summary of web-search results, gathered by automated agents and limited in what it could reach. It does not replace a consultation with a qualified doctor. It also does not replace your own checks of a doctor's licence and credentials.

### How the research was done, and its limits

- **Search summaries only.** Almost every fact comes from web-search result summaries, not from the pages themselves. Opening ishrs.org failed because the site was not on the session's allowed network list. Opening fda.gov failed for the same kind of reason. The ABHRS "Find My Surgeon" tool and the IAHRS location filter were not queried directly.
- **The search budget ran out.** All agents in the run shared a limit of 200 web searches, and it was used up partway through. Many tracks returned nothing:
  - **Regions.** The first pass found no doctors for Texas and the Southwest, the West Coast, Canada, the UK and Europe, Turkey, Asia-Pacific, or Latin America, Australia and the Middle East. A later rerun found 67 doctors in California and the Pacific Northwest, Arizona, Texas, Canada, the UK, Spain, Germany, Japan, Korea, India, Singapore, Mexico, Colombia, the UAE, Israel and Turkey. Even then, the requested verification checks were only partly possible: abhrs.org has no diplomate registry that search could reach.
  - **Still not covered.** Tennessee, Orlando, Jacksonville and the Tampa area. In the Midwest, only Chicago and Ohio were searched. Minnesota, Michigan, Missouri, Wisconsin and Indiana were not searched, and neither were academic hair clinics in the Midwest.
  - **Specialist areas not covered.** Women's hair loss specialists, CCCA and traction alopecia experts, and repair specialists.
  - **Treatments not covered.** FDA approval status of standard drugs, new antiandrogens, new mechanisms, JAK inhibitor labels for alopecia areata, PRP and exosomes, follicle cloning and bioengineering, devices, surgical advances, and pipeline hype checks.
- **"Most-published" rankings are not citation metrics.** No bibliometric tool such as Scopus or Web of Science was available. Rankings rest on publication counts that the doctors report themselves, and sources disagree on those counts.
- **Clinical trial investigators were not checked on ClinicalTrials.gov.** Investigator roles mostly come from authorship of key papers, sponsor press releases and institutional pages.

### Corrections and open points

- **Correction on oral minoxidil evidence in women.** The randomized trial of low-dose oral minoxidil in women is Ramos et al. (JAAD 2020), co-authored by Rodney Sinclair. Vañó-Galván's paper (JAAD 2021) is a retrospective safety study of 1,404 patients, not a randomized trial. (unverified: the notes give no source links)
- **What is approved for women.** Topical minoxidil is the only FDA-approved drug for women's pattern hair loss. Spironolactone, oral minoxidil, oral or topical finasteride, dutasteride and hydroxychloroquine are all used off-label in women. (unverified)
- **2026 ISHRS awards.** The 34th ISHRS World Congress ran October 15-17, 2026 in Rio de Janeiro ([ishrs.org](https://ishrs.org/ishrs-world-congress-2026/)). The researchers found no announcement of the 2026 Golden Follicle, Platinum Follicle or Distinguished Assistant winners.
- **ISHRS faculty lists.** The full faculty lists are in the [2025 ISHRS program guide (PDF)](https://ishrs.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/ishrs_programguide_web_2025-10-16.pdf), which was not read. Maxim Chumak is reported as program chair of the 2026 Scientific Planning Committee. (unverified)
- **NAHRS has a new name.** NAHRS now operates as the American Hair Research Society ([americanhairresearchsociety.org](https://americanhairresearchsociety.org)).
