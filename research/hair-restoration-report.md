## 1. Bottom line

- **What works now for pattern thinning** is the established medical base: minoxidil plus 5-alpha-reductase inhibitors. A JAMA Dermatology 2022 network meta-analysis (Gupta et al.; 23 studies) ranked dutasteride 0.5 mg highest at 24 weeks and finasteride 5 mg highest at 48 weeks. The editorial on that analysis warns against over-reading the rankings.
- **Low-dose oral minoxidil (LDOM)** is widely prescribed off-label (US). It is mainly a convenience and tolerability option, not proven to work better than topical minoxidil.
  - Safety looks acceptable in a 1,404-patient series: hypertrichosis 15.1%, and 1.7% stopped because of side effects.
  - Two RCTs found it no better than topical minoxidil: 5 mg vs topical 5% in 90 men (JAMA Derm 2024), and 1 mg vs topical 5% in 52 women.
  - It needs physician supervision with blood pressure and heart rate monitoring.
- **Hair transplant surgery (FUT/FUE, including robotic FUE)** is the established surgical option. Choose the surgeon on credentials you can check, and confirm the surgeon personally performs the operation (see section 4).
- **For alopecia areata (patchy or total autoimmune loss, not pattern thinning)**, JAK inhibitors are the real breakthrough of this decade. Baricitinib was FDA-approved in June 2022 and deuruxolitinib (Leqselvi) in July 2024. Ritlecitinib is described in the material as the third FDA-approved program, but that was not confirmed.
- **Coming in 2026-2028 for pattern hair loss**, none of these is approved yet:
  - **Clascoterone 5% scalp solution**: positive Phase 3 per the company; NDA and EU application planned.
  - **VDPHL01 extended-release oral minoxidil**: two positive Phase 3 readouts in men per the company; NDA expected; women's trial reads out H1 2027.
  - **Earlier stage**: GT20029 (Phase II) and PP405 (Phase 2a).
  - Almost all efficacy figures are company-reported and not peer-reviewed.
- **Who to see:**
  - **Diagnosis and medical therapy**, especially for women, scarring alopecia or alopecia areata: an academic hair-disorders dermatologist, for example Tosti (Miami), Jerry Shapiro or Lo Sicco (NYU), Senna (Lahey/Boston), Hordinsky (Minnesota), McMichael (Wake Forest), Aguh (Johns Hopkins) or Cotsarelis (Penn).
  - **Surgery**: a surgeon whose ABHRS Diplomate status and/or ISHRS Fellow (FISHRS) status you have confirmed in the official directories. Peer-award winners and society leaders are listed in section 3.
- **Be sceptical of paid directories, commercial "top surgeon" rankings, clinic self-descriptions and relative-percent efficacy claims.** All of these appear in the material.

### Proven now
- **Pattern hair loss:** topical minoxidil, finasteride or dutasteride (ranked in the 2022 network meta-analysis), and surgical transplantation (FUT/FUE) in suitable candidates.
- **Severe alopecia areata:** baricitinib (2022) and deuruxolitinib (2024) are FDA-approved. Ritlecitinib is reported as approved but was not confirmed.

### Strong newer options
- **LDOM (off-label):** large safety series, but RCTs show it is *not superior* to topical minoxidil. Its main advantage is convenience and adherence.
- **LDOM + spironolactone for women and sublingual minoxidil:** promising but small studies, and the lead investigator has a patent conflict of interest.
- **LDOM in breast-cancer survivors:** small NYU cohort, reassuring so far.

### Pipeline 2026-2028
- **Clascoterone 5%:** filing planned; the nearest new topical anti-androgen.
- **VDPHL01:** NDA expected after Studies 302 and 304; women's data due H1 2027.
- **GT20029:** heading to Phase III and a US Phase II.
- **PP405:** Phase 2, including a women's trial.
- **KX-826:** Phase III, outcome disputed.
- **Prostaglandin D2 / GPR44:** a drug target identified by the Cotsarelis lab (Sci Transl Med 2012). The material names no specific drug.
- **Alopecia areata expansion:** pediatric and adolescent JAK trials (baricitinib NCT05723198; adolescent deuruxolitinib), a two-dose ritlecitinib Phase 3, and multicytokine-inhibitor trials (Sinclair site).

### Hype or unproven, and why
- **PP405 efficacy claims** circulating on clinic websites (31%, "AWAKEN"): there is no primary source, and the only public data are safety data.
- **KX-826 "primary endpoint met":** contradicted by another report. Treat it as unresolved.
- **Clascoterone "539%" headlines:** these are relative-to-vehicle percentages, the figures cannot be assigned to a specific trial, and they are not peer-reviewed.
- **PRP and LLLT as marketed:** the material contains no controlled evidence of benefit.
- **"Hair cloning":** follicle-cell culture appears only as lab research (for example Cooley's early work). The material contains no clinical product.
- **"Best surgeon" lists:** the Spex Hair ranking is commercial. IAHRS and the Coalition are paid, curated listings; the Hair Transplant Network publisher confirmed sponsorship fees.

## 3. Top doctors

### Ranking criteria (objective signals used)
1. **Peer awards** from the ISHRS: Platinum Follicle (research and innovation), Golden Follicle (clinical and education) and the Lifetime Achievement Award.
2. **Leadership roles** in ISHRS, ABHRS or the American Hair Research Society (AHRS, formerly NAHRS).
3. **Certification and fellowship:** ABHRS Diplomate (exam plus case logs; ABHRS is **not** an ABMS board) and FISHRS (an honour, not a certification).
4. **Research leadership:** first or lead author or PI on pivotal trials and landmark papers.
5. **Academic appointment** and direction of a dedicated hair clinic.

**Limits of these signals:**
- None of them measures patient outcomes.
- All officer titles may be out of date: new ISHRS officers took over at the October 15-17, 2026 Rio Congress, and the 2026 award winners were not found.
- Every row below is not independently verified.

## 4. How to choose a surgeon, and red flags (from the material only)

**Check credentials yourself:**
- ABHRS "Find a Diplomate" directory at abhrs.org/directory. ABHRS is not an ABMS board, so certificationmatters.org will not show it.
- The ISHRS find-a-doctor directory.
- The state medical-licence registry (GMC for the UK; CPSO for Ontario).

**Wording that is itself a red flag:** ABHRS ethics rules require "ABHRS Diplomate". A clinic advertising "board-certified by the ABHRS" is breaking that rule.

**Know what the credentials mean:**
- FISHRS is an honour, not a certification.
- ISHRS membership is a professional-society membership.
- IAHRS (invitation, review and unannounced inspections) and the Coalition (requires sessions of 3,000+ grafts and open critique of results) are **paid, curated listings**, not independent rankings.

**Ask who does the surgery.** ISHRS campaigns (Fight the FIGHT, the Committee on the Unlicensed Practice of Medicine) and surgeons such as Unger, Mejia, Keene and Tykocinski specifically warn about black-market clinics and unlicensed (non-physician) surgery. Confirm the physician personally performs the critical steps.

**Check whether a famous name still operates.** Several senior surgeons are listed as ISHRS Emeritus (for example Bernstein and True).

**Discount marketing claims:** procedure counts (for example "5,000+"), self-described "first" or "pioneer" claims, commercial "top 25" lists, and clinic blogs used as sources.

**Ask about industry ties** for anyone recommending a specific drug: for example King (Lilly), Christiano (Aclaris), Hordinsky (AbbVie) and Sinclair (minoxidil patents).

**Not covered in the material:** specific warnings about unrealistic graft-count promises, and patient-outcome or complication data for named surgeons.

## 5. Caveats and what to verify before booking

- **Not medical advice.** Hair loss has many causes (pattern, alopecia areata, scarring alopecias, telogen effluvium). Get a diagnosis, and a scalp biopsy if a dermatologist advises one, before choosing a treatment.
- **Nothing here was independently verified.** Before relying on any credential, award or title, check it on the primary page (ishrs.org, abhrs.org, a university profile).
- **Officer titles are probably out of date.** The ISHRS Congress was October 15-17, 2026, so roles held by von Albertini, Unger, Mejia, Williams and Speranzini may have changed. The 2026 award winners were not found.
- **Unresolved conflicts:**
  - 2025 Lifetime Achievement Award: Wolf vs Martinick.
  - 2022 Golden Follicle: Lam vs Krugluger.
  - 2024 Platinum Follicle: Boudjema vs clinic claims.
  - Ron Shapiro's Golden Follicle year.
  - Bergfeld's NAHRS dates.
  - McMichael's chair status.
  - Gupta's location.
  - Umar's office city.
- **Pipeline numbers** (clascoterone, VDPHL01, PP405, GT20029, KX-826) are mostly topline company data. Expect changes after peer review and FDA review, and none is approved.
- **LDOM is off-label.** Discuss blood pressure, heart rate and fluid-retention monitoring with the prescriber.
- **JAK inhibitors are for alopecia areata, not pattern thinning.**
- **Before booking, confirm:** current address, whether the doctor accepts insurance (some NYU hair specialists reportedly do not), who performs each surgical step, and how complications and repairs are handled.
